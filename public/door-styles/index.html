IXInsulation

Insulation & R-value

Insulation keeps an attached garage more comfortable, makes the door quieter and adds strength. Polypack doors are rated R-8 and Steel Back doors are rated R-10.

Non-insulated

A single layer of steel. The most budget-friendly option, fine for detached garages and storage.

Polypack

Polystyrene insulation added behind the steel. Keeps the garage more comfortable and makes the door quieter. Rated R-8.

Steel Back

A 2" thick, 3-layer sandwich: steel outside, insulation in the middle, steel inside. The strongest, quietest and best-insulated option. Rated R-10.

  • 2" thick, 3-layer insulated steel sandwich panel
  • CFC-free polystyrene insulation that won't break down over time
  • Tongue-and-groove joints between sections to seal out wind and rain
  • Heavy-duty galvanized stiles for hinges, rollers and the opener
  • Bottom weatherstrip in a rigid retainer, easy to replace

Door style questions

What does R-value mean?

R-value measures how well insulation slows heat moving through the door. The higher the number, the better it insulates. Polypack doors are rated R-8 and Steel Back doors are rated R-10. Either keeps an attached garage noticeably cooler in summer and warmer in winter, and makes the door quieter.

Do I need an insulated garage door?

If your garage is attached to the house, has a room above it, or you use it as a workshop or gym, insulation is worth it. For a detached garage used only for parking or storage, a non-insulated door is usually fine.

What's the difference between 24 and 26 gauge steel?

Gauge measures steel thickness, and a lower number means thicker steel. 24 gauge is thicker and more dent-resistant than 26 gauge.

Can I get windows in my new door?

Yes. Most styles can have windows, from a row along the top to modern side windows or full-view glass, with clear, frosted, obscure or tinted glass.
